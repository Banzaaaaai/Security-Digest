CRITICAL · Priority 95.0

Rackspace Spot breach via compromised credentials exposed customer Kubernetes secrets

US 85% · Europe 60% · MEA 40% · Other 40% · Global IT services 95% · Energy services 20% · Zero Trust and cloud security 85% · Identity and privileged access 75%

Rackspace says attackers used compromised credentials to breach its Rackspace Spot cloud platform. They deployed unauthorized workloads and accessed Kubernetes secrets in customer clusters.

Why it matters: A major breach at a US cloud and managed-services provider (a target industry), with possible downstream exposure of customer secrets.

Action: Rackspace Spot customers should rotate every Kubernetes secret and credential stored in their clusters. They should also audit for unauthorized workloads and check downstream systems for misuse of those secrets.

Detection assessment: As an assessment, Kubernetes audit logs covering secret reads and unexpected pods, plus monitoring of how exposed credentials are used, are the key ways to detect related activity.

Sources: BleepingComputer

FBI reportedly suffers second breach in two months, this time involving NCIC police database

US 100% · Europe 20% · MEA 10% · Other 10% · Government 95% · Global IT services 15% · Data protection and DLP 60% · Identity and privileged access 45%

Reports say FBI systems were breached again, less than two months after an earlier incident. This time the reported target is the National Crime Information Center (NCIC), which local, state and federal law enforcement use to share information. The source does not confirm the scope, the method or who is responsible.

Why it matters: A major reported breach at a US federal government agency (a target industry and region) involving a nationwide law-enforcement data system. The breach is reported but not yet confirmed.

Action: Agencies connected to NCIC should review access logs and account privileges for this system and follow any official FBI or CJIS guidance. Treat the breach details as unconfirmed until officials disclose more.

Detection assessment: The attack method was not disclosed, so no specific detections can be recommended. As a general assessment, agencies should watch for unusual access to shared criminal-justice systems and for credential misuse.

HIGH · Priority 79.0

Microsoft reports breach after flaw in Entra Connect Health exploited to access tenant data

US 90% · Europe 90% · MEA 85% · Other 85% · Global IT services 70% · Government 60% · Identity and privileged access 90% · Zero Trust and cloud security 85%

Microsoft says it suffered a data breach after attackers exploited a vulnerability in its Entra Connect Health service. The attackers got unauthorized access to customers' administrator email addresses and tenant configuration information. No CVE, scope or attribution was given in the source.

Why it matters: No source-reported exploitation or recent KEV evidence; vulnerability capped at high.

Action: Check Microsoft notifications to see whether your tenant was affected. Warn administrators about targeted phishing, enforce phishing-resistant MFA for admin accounts, and review tenant configuration for exposed details.

Detection assessment: This was exploited on Microsoft's side, so customers likely have little visibility. As an assessment, the leaked admin emails make targeted phishing likely, so tune detection for phishing against admins.

HIGH · Priority 75.75

Researchers show Ghostscript sandbox escape enabling RCE through crafted file uploads

US 80% · Europe 80% · MEA 80% · Other 80% · Global IT services 60% · Government 50% · Vulnerability and exposure management 85% · Secure software delivery and DevSecOps 65%

pwn.ai showed that a now-patched Ghostscript flaw lets a crafted file escape the sandbox and run commands on servers that process uploads. Many Linux applications call Ghostscript indirectly to convert PDFs, PostScript files and images. No in-the-wild exploitation was reported.

Why it matters: A remote code execution flaw in widely used server-side software, with a public demonstration and a patch available. No exploitation has been reported.

Action: Find Ghostscript in your environment, including where it is pulled in indirectly, and apply the patched version. Isolate file-conversion services.

Detection assessment: Assessment: watch for unexpected child processes started by gs or by image and document conversion workers.

Sources: The Hacker News

HIGH · Priority 75.5

Months-long prompt theft found at three AI inference providers; OpenRouter drops them

US 70% · Europe 65% · MEA 55% · Other 55% · Global IT services 65% · Private banking 40% · AI-assisted work and AI governance 90% · Data protection and DLP 80%

Dragos reported that attackers stole data from inside three AI inference providers for months without the providers noticing. Some of the stolen requests were marked Zero Data Retention. OpenRouter removed the three providers from its routing pool.

Why it matters: A significant supply-chain data exposure in AI services that undermines assurances about zero data retention.

Action: Review which AI routing services and inference providers you use. Assume sensitive prompts may have been exposed, and rotate any secrets included in prompts.

Detection assessment: Assessment: customers have limited visibility into provider-side theft, so detection depends on vendor assurance.

Sources: The Hacker News
